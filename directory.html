Service area Crystal River watershed

Protect and defend the Crystal River through vigilant grassroots action, and maintain and improve water quality and quantity.

Volunteer grassroots organization with an executive committee of three and a board of nine. PCHR protects, defends and enhances the rivers and streams of the Roaring Fork Watershed on behalf of the people and environment.

Safeguards water quality and quantity, secures in-stream flow rights, and leads restoration, education and advocacy projects that strengthen the valley's resilience to drought and climate change. Roaring Fork ConservancyRoaring Fork watershed

Service area Roaring Fork watershed

Inspiring people to explore, value, and protect the Roaring Fork Watershed.

Founded in 1996, one of the largest watershed organizations in Colorado. A trusted local resource providing school and community-based Watershed Education programs, watershed science to inform policy projects and decisions, regional watershed planning, water resource policy initiatives, and stream management and restoration. RFC is a water information clearinghouse within the Roaring Fork Watershed, matching people with concerns to the right resources. They are also a liaison between regulatory agencies and the public, translating complex water issues to the community. Along with other groups they are trying to keep as much water in the Roaring Fork watershed as possible. Holds conservation easements adjacent to the rivers.

Funded by
501(c)(3) nonprofit, funded mainly through contributions, grants and program fees.

roaringfork.org

RWAPARoaring Fork watershed

Service area Roaring Fork watershed

RWAPA was created to address water, power, recreational, environmental and related land issues with respect to Ruedi Reservoir, the largest major water supply and recreational reservoir within the Roaring Fork Watershed, and other local water resources pursuant to an intergovernmental agreement.

Ruedi Water and Power Authority is a consortium of local governments within the Roaring Fork watershed supporting comprehensive water planning for the watershed. RWAPA regularly convenes the Watershed Collaborative to advance the commitments of the Roaring Fork Watershed Plan. RWAPA works in tandem with Colorado Parks and Wildlife, the US Forest Service and valley stakeholders to promote an outreach campaign to mitigate the spread of invasive mussels, and works with the Bureau of Reclamation to manage releases from the reservoir to lower temperatures in the river and provide water for agricultural, municipal and environmental uses.

Funded by
Municipal and county contributions, grants and intergovernmental agreements.

Colorado Basin RoundtableColorado River BasinOffers funding

Service area Colorado River Basin

Managing, protecting and sustaining water resources within the Colorado River Basin. Goals are to protect, conserve and develop water supplies within the Western Slope of Colorado for future needs.

Established in 2005 by the Colorado Legislature, the CBRT is a group of water managers and stakeholders who work to solve water-related issues within the Colorado River Basin, from its headwaters in Rocky Mountain National Park to the Utah state line. In addition to providing information and action, the CBRT is a source of funding for numerous and varied water projects within the basin. Monthly meetings are open to the public.

Funded by
Through the Colorado Water Conservation Board.

Colorado River District15 West Slope countiesOffers funding

Service area 15 West Slope counties

To lead in the protection, conservation, use and development of the water resources of the Colorado River basin for the welfare of the District, and to safeguard for Colorado all waters of the Colorado River to which the state is entitled.

Serves water users across the 15 West Slope Colorado counties through legal and legislative advocacy, technical and engineering support, community outreach and education, and multi-benefit water project grant funding. In addition to protecting West Slope water interests within District borders, the CRD also supports the State of Colorado in ensuring its water interests within the greater Colorado River Basin are protected during federal and interstate negotiations. Quarterly board meetings are open to the public. Formally the Colorado River Water Conservation District, commonly referred to as 'the District'.

Funded by
The budgets of the 15 Western Slope counties that make up the District.
Offers funding
the Community Funding Partnership, created in 2021, funds multi-purpose water projects on the Western Slope across five categories: productive agriculture, infrastructure, healthy rivers, watershed health and water quality, and conservation and efficiency. These funds help projects attract matching money from state, federal and private sources.

Colorado Water Conservation BoardStatewideOffers funding

Service area Statewide

To conserve, develop, protect and manage Colorado's water for present and future generations.

The CWCB is the state's primary water policy agency, serving as a resource for technical assistance, planning and funding to ensure Colorado's waters benefit present and future generations. Established by the Colorado Legislature in 1937 to aid in the protection and development of the waters of the state, the CWCB has evolved from a focus on water development and defense of Colorado's water rights to a broader role that includes conservation, watershed protection, drought planning, flood mitigation, water supply planning and collaborative statewide water policy.

Funded by
Almost entirely self-funded through the CWCB Construction Fund, a revolving fund created in 1971 to provide low-interest loans for Colorado water projects.
Offers funding
available to water providers and other entities statewide for water-related projects, studies, planning documents, awareness campaigns and other activities.

cwcb.colorado.gov

Division of Water Resources (DWR)Roaring Fork watershed - Water District 38, split near Emma

Service area Roaring Fork watershed - Water District 38, split near Emma

The Division of Water Resources administers the waters of the State to maximize lawful beneficial use, ensures that dams and water wells are properly constructed and safe, and provides information about water resources to the public.

The Division of Water Resources, also known as the Office of the State Engineer, administers water rights, issues water well permits, represents Colorado in interstate water compact proceedings, monitors streamflow and water use, approves construction and repair of dams and performs dam safety inspections, licenses well drillers, and maintains numerous databases of Colorado water information. Division 5, where all of Colorado's major transmountain diversion projects originate, includes the Roaring Fork Watershed as District 38. District 38 has two water commissioners, split near Emma: one covers Emma upstream on the Roaring Fork, the other covers Emma downstream plus the Crystal River. They work mainly in the field, educating water users and inspecting and measuring ditch flows, as they control the headgates of the ditches. The website offers public tools including a way to track what is being diverted from the river.

Funded by
State government.

dwr.colorado.gov

Basalt Water Conservancy DistrictEagle, Garfield & Pitkin counties

Service area Eagle, Garfield & Pitkin counties

To conserve, develop and stabilize supplies of water for domestic, irrigation, manufacturing and other beneficial uses within those parts of Eagle, Garfield and Pitkin counties included within its boundaries.

The BWCD holds various water rights decrees resulting from adjudications connected with the Basalt Project, whose principal feature is Ruedi Reservoir above Basalt. The District holds contracts for storage rights in Ruedi Reservoir and Green Mountain Reservoir as well as direct flow rights, which it uses to secure dependable water supplies for water users within its boundaries. These rights provide year-round water right protection to its constituents. In most cases, those contracting with the District obtain their physical water supply from groundwater wells or springs in the Roaring Fork and Fryingpan river basins. The standard homeowner contract is 100 gallons per person per day. Monthly meetings are open to the public.

Funded by
Budget of approximately $150,000, funded mainly through water contracts plus a small amount from mill levy taxes.

Mid Valley Metropolitan DistrictBasalt, El Jebel & nearby unincorporated areas

Service area Basalt, El Jebel & nearby unincorporated areas

To provide water, wastewater and recreation services to the developing center of the Roaring Fork Valley.

Mid Valley Metropolitan District is a Colorado special district formed in 1983. In 2003 the District completed a new 500,000 gallon per day extended aeration wastewater facility, and added a second basin in 2014; the facility can treat up to 999,000 gallons per day and is expected to handle projected growth until approximately 2030. Potable water comes from a series of wells throughout the Mid Valley area, with storage in several tanks on the surrounding hillsides providing over 1.5 million gallons. Serves areas of the Town of Basalt, the El Jebel area, and other unincorporated subdivisions within Eagle and Garfield counties.

Funded by
Special district - user rates and taxes.

Snowmass Water and Sanitation DistrictSnowmass VillageOffers funding

Service area Snowmass Village

To provide customers with high quality water every day of the year. Water delivered to homes in Snowmass Village surpasses the strict regulations set by the State of Colorado and the US EPA. Safety and security are top priorities.

Snowmass Water and Sanitation District was formed in 1968 to serve the new ski area, before the Town was created. It is responsible for the public water system and wastewater treatment system serving Snowmass Village. The District is governed by a publicly elected five-member board of directors; staff implement the policies and procedures set by the Board and conduct daily operations, including monitoring irrigation. East Snowmass Creek delivers 96% of Snowmass water. Monthly board meetings are open to the public.

Funded by
Property taxes and user rates, funding infrastructure upgrades and operations.
Offers funding
rebates available to homeowners for water saving efforts.

Town of BasaltMunicipal water system

Service area Municipal water system

Municipal water treatment, storage and distribution for the Town of Basalt.

The Town-owned municipal water system provides water treatment, storage and distribution to approximately 925 service connections. The Town of Basalt Water Department serves east Basalt, including Old Town, Southside and Elk Run. The primary sources are Basalt Springs and Lucksinger Springs on Basalt Mountain, piped into town and filtered. Three deep wells drawing from the Roaring Fork aquifer supplement the spring water during periods of high demand, such as the summer irrigation season.

West Divide Water Conservancy DistrictExtends west and north beyond the watershed

Service area Extends west and north beyond the watershed

To provide water right augmentation supplies within the District's service areas for all beneficial uses, and to promote the health, safety and welfare of the public while providing such service.

West Divide has decreed several regional water right plans for augmentation that provide a legal supply of water to its constituents. This legal supply allows contractees to continue using water when they are out of priority, without being curtailed or having to go through a lengthy water court process to decree their own augmentation plan. Water is released from upstream reservoirs - Ruedi, Green Mountain and Wolford Mountain - to replace water used by District contractees who are out of priority. West Divide is also involved in water supply planning for existing and future demands within the District, and in monitoring and managing water quality. A typical contract is 350 gallons per home per day.

Funded by
Water allotment contracts.

wdwcd.org
